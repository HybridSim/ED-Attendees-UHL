Background: The operational MIDCARE model provides a site-specific, dynamic view of emergency care demand and trolley pressure at UHL. It is designed to complement ESRI’s regional, population-based projections by making key assumptions explicit and enabling scenario exploration at the level of a single Model 4 hospital.

Methods: Historic ED attendance data for UHL from 2014–2025 are used as a baseline for a compound annual growth projection to 2075. Trolley demand is derived from ED attendances, an ED-to-admission conversion rate, and average length of stay. Capacity is modelled as a separate growth process and can be soft-linked to demand growth. Critical mass thresholds (multiples of K) are identified algorithmically and displayed as vertical markers. All outputs are dynamically updated when the user adjusts the parameter sliders.

Gaps filled relative to ESRI: ESRI projections are regional, scenario-based estimates anchored to 2023 utilisation patterns and are not designed to forecast UHL attendances directly. The MIDCARE dashboard fills this gap by (i) using observed UHL ED activity, (ii) visualising the time path of crowding and capacity shortfall, (iii) highlighting critical-mass exceedance years that ESRI and HIQA do not quantify, and (iv) making key assumptions transparent and adjustable, as recommended by HIQA.

Results: The model generates projected trajectories for ED attendances, trolley demand, and bed capacity at UHL, along with a shortfall series and a set of critical-mass crossing years. For plausible parameter settings, the dashboard illustrates how compounded demand growth and constrained capacity can lead to persistent overcrowding and recurrent breaches of critical thresholds.

Conclusions: This is a dynamic simulation model rather than a static workbook. By exposing assumptions and enabling scenario testing, it operationalises HIQA’s advice that ESRI projections should be interpreted alongside observed demand and site-level analyses. The dashboard provides a decision-support tool for aligning long-term capacity planning with the acute operational realities at UHL.

A1.1 Purpose of this annex

This annex is provided to support decision-making by clarifying how ESRI capacity projections should be interpreted when considered alongside observed emergency department (ED) attendance trends at University Hospital Limerick (UHL). It should be read in conjunction with Section 3 (Current demand and capacity), Section 5 (Future demand and capacity requirements) and Section 7 (Risks and implications for patient safety) of the main report.

This annex accompanies the operational dashboard to ensure that site-level data are interpreted consistently with the strategic evidence base used in this review.

A1.2 ESRI projections: scope and intended use

As outlined in Section 5, the ESRI projections referenced in this review are population-based, scenario-driven estimates intended to inform long-term planning for acute hospital capacity.

The Advice document states:

“The ESRI analyses to inform future service needs project ever-growing demand for services in the Mid West to 2040.”
Advice to the Minister for Health to inform decision-making around the design and delivery of urgent and emergency healthcare services in the HSE Mid West, p.9

The ESRI estimates that:

“The number of urgent and emergency care presentations at hospitals in the Mid West will increase by between 16% and 23% between 2023 and 2040.”
Advice to the Minister for Health, p.29

These projections are designed to support strategic planning rather than to forecast site-specific emergency department attendances.

HIQA explicitly cautions that projections must be interpreted alongside observed demand:

“It is essential that there is ongoing monitoring of actual demand for services relative to the ESRI capacity projections.”
Advice to the Minister for Health, p.10

A1.3 Observed emergency department attendances at UHL

As set out in Section 3, UHL is the sole Model 4 hospital in the Mid West and therefore absorbs a disproportionately high volume of urgent and emergency care demand.

HIQA notes explicitly:

“The Mid West has the highest number of ED attendances of any Model 4 hospital.”
Advice to the Minister for Health, p.9

The operational dashboard accompanying this review is based on observed ED attendance data at UHL from 2014 to 2025. These data show an increase from approximately 55,500 attendances in 2014 to over 95,000 attendances by 2025, representing growth of approximately 72% over an 11-year period.

The Expert Advisory Group cautioned against relying on standardised metrics alone when interpreting such demand:

“Presenting only the per capita figures without the absolute figures may be misleading… when in absolute terms, [UHL] actually has a higher number of presentations than other Model 4 hospitals.”
Expert Advisory Group – Membership and Minutes, p.31 of 32

A1.4 Key differences between strategic projections and operational analysis

The ESRI projections and the operational dashboard address different but complementary questions.

The Expert Advisory Group explicitly recognised limitations in current data and modelling approaches in capturing ED operational pressure:

“Improved qualitative and quantitative measures for overcrowding in the ED are needed.”
Expert Advisory Group – Membership and Minutes, p.18 of 32

In addition, the Group acknowledged that:

“Comparing Model 4 hospitals is imperfect due to the structural differences.”
Expert Advisory Group – Membership and Minutes, p.18 of 32

These structural differences are particularly relevant in the Mid West, where a single ED serves a large and growing catchment.

A1.5 Implications for interpretation and decision-making

HIQA’s analysis concludes that current demand already exceeds available capacity at UHL:

“Data available to HIQA shows that demand for urgent and emergency healthcare services and inpatient care continues to outpace available bed capacity at UHL.”
Advice to the Minister for Health, p.29

When considered alongside observed ED attendance growth at UHL, this indicates that ESRI projections—while essential for long-term planning—do not fully describe the scale or immediacy of operational pressure at the site.

Accordingly, ESRI projections should be interpreted as planning envelopes rather than forecasts of UHL ED attendances, and should be complemented by site-specific monitoring and operational analysis.

A1.6 Conclusion

This annex supports the conclusion set out in Section 8 (Conclusions and advice) that decisions regarding urgent and emergency healthcare services in the Mid West should be informed by both strategic projections and observed site-level evidence.

The dashboard provides an operational complement to ESRI projections and reinforces the need for continued monitoring of ED attendances and demand–capacity mismatch at UHL, alongside long-term planning to address future service needs.
