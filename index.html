  • This dashboard is an operational simulation of emergency care demand at University Hospital Limerick (UHL). It is intended to complement, not replace, ESRI population-based projections included in HIQA's review. This tool is released as a beta version for scenario exploration.
  • Historic UHL ED attendances (2014–2025) are used as observed baseline. Projections begin in 2026.
  • Trolley demand is derived from ED attendances, an ED admission (conversion) rate and average length of stay. Acute bed capacity growth is modelled separately and can be linked to or decoupled from demand growth.
  • Dark red vertical markers indicate critical-mass thresholds (multiples of K), increments of demand equal to K beyond the last observed ED attendance, identifying step-changes in scale rather than precise capacity limits.
  • Projection horizon extends to 2075 to illustrate long-run system behaviour; critical-mass thresholds are shown only within a strategic planning horizon to 2050 to avoid over-interpretation of long-run exponential effects.

Background: The operational MIDCARE model provides a site-specific, dynamic view of emergency care demand and trolley pressure at UHL. It is intended to complement ESRI’s regional, population-based projections included in HIQA's review on UEC in the MW, by making key assumptions explicit and enabling scenario exploration at the level of a single Model 4 hospital. ESRI projections are regional, scenario-based estimates anchored to baseline utilisation patterns and are not designed to forecast UHL attendances directly.

Methods: Historic ED attendance data for UHL from 2014 to 2025 are used as observed baseline, with projections commencing from 2026. Trolley demand is derived from ED attendances, an ED admission (conversion) rate and average length of stay. Capacity is modelled as a separate growth process and can be linked to or decoupled from demand growth. Critical mass thresholds (multiples of K) are identified and displayed as vertical markers to 2050.

Results: The model generates projected trajectories for ED attendances, trolley demand and capacity, along with a shortfall series and critical-mass crossing years. For plausible parameter settings, the dashboard illustrates how compounded demand growth and constrained capacity can produce persistent overcrowding and recurrent breaches of critical thresholds.

Conclusions: This is a dynamic simulation model rather than a static model. By exposing assumptions and enabling scenario testing, it operationalises HIQA’s advice that ESRI projections should be interpreted alongside observed demand and site-level analyses. The dashboard is intended as decision-support to align long-term capacity planning with operational realities at UHL.

A1.1 Purpose of this annex

This annex supports interpretation of ESRI capacity projections alongside observed emergency department attendance trends at UHL. It is provided to ensure that operational, site-level data are interpreted consistently with the strategic evidence base used in the HIQA Mid West review.

A1.2 ESRI projections: scope and intended use

The ESRI projections referenced in HIQA’s review are population-based, scenario-driven estimates intended to inform long-term planning for acute hospital capacity. They are regional planning estimates and are not designed to forecast site-specific ED attendances at UHL.

“The ESRI analyses to inform future service needs project ever-growing demand for services in the Mid West to 2040.”
Source: HIQA Advice to the Minister for Health (Urgent and emergency healthcare services, HSE Mid West), p.9.
“The number of urgent and emergency care presentations at hospitals in the Mid West will increase by between 16% and 23% between 2023 and 2040.”
Source: HIQA Advice to the Minister for Health (Urgent and emergency healthcare services, HSE Mid West), p.29.
“It is essential that there is ongoing monitoring of actual demand for services relative to the ESRI capacity projections.”
Source: HIQA Advice to the Minister for Health (Urgent and emergency healthcare services, HSE Mid West), p.10.

A1.3 Observed emergency department attendances at UHL

UHL is the primary Model 4 hospital in the Mid West and absorbs a high volume of urgent and emergency care demand. The dashboard baseline uses observed ED attendances (2014–2025) to illustrate the scale and trajectory of demand at the site level.

“The Mid West has the highest number of ED attendances of any Model 4 hospital.”
Source: HIQA Advice to the Minister for Health (Urgent and emergency healthcare services, HSE Mid West), p.9.
“Presenting only the per capita figures without the absolute figures may be misleading… when in absolute terms, [UHL] actually has a higher number of presentations than other Model 4 hospitals.”
Source: HIQA Expert Advisory Group – Membership and Minutes, p.31 of 32.

A1.4 Key differences between strategic projections and operational analysis

The ESRI projections and the operational dashboard address different but complementary questions. ESRI projections support strategic, regional planning. The dashboard supports operational scenario exploration anchored in observed UHL activity and makes assumptions explicit.

“Improved qualitative and quantitative measures for overcrowding in the ED are needed.”
Source: HIQA Expert Advisory Group – Membership and Minutes, p.18 of 32.
“Comparing Model 4 hospitals is imperfect due to the structural differences.”
Source: HIQA Expert Advisory Group – Membership and Minutes, p.18 of 32.

A1.5 Implications for interpretation and decision-making

HIQA evidence indicates that demand for urgent and emergency care and inpatient capacity at UHL remains mismatched. Accordingly, ESRI projections should be interpreted as planning envelopes and complemented by site-level monitoring and operational analysis.

“Data available to HIQA shows that demand for urgent and emergency healthcare services and inpatient care continues to outpace available bed capacity at UHL.”
Source: HIQA Advice to the Minister for Health (Urgent and emergency healthcare services, HSE Mid West), p.29.

A1.6 Conclusion

The dashboard serves a distinct but complementary purposes to HIQA's review. Strategic planning should be informed by ESRI regional projections, while operational planning and risk management require ongoing monitoring of observed UHL ED attendances and demand–capacity mismatch at the site level.
